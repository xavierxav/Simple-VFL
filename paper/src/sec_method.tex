\section{Operating Regimes and Cost Accounting}\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Setting and threat model.}
$K$ parties hold different images $x_i^{(k)}$ of the same $N$ entities $i$, aligned beforehand, e.g.\ by private set intersection. The label owner, the \emph{server}, holds the labels and may also be a party. Raw images never leave their owner, and the encoder $f$ is public. The server is honest but curious: it tries to recover the parties' images or link uploads to them, knowing $f$, public images of the domain and, at worst, copies of the candidates.

\paragraph{R1: one upload.}\phantomsection\label{sec:r1}
Each party computes $z_i^{(k)}=f(x_i^{(k)})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$, the pooled output token of a frozen encoder ($d=384$ to $1536$), averaged over the images it holds of an entity (revisits, or rotated copies of a photograph), and sends it \emph{once}. The server trains a head on their concatenation for as many epochs as it wants: a two-layer MLP, or on MILK10k \emph{late fusion} of one MLP per party. \emph{Local compression:} each party fits a PCA on its own training features, keeps $d'$ components and quantises each one uniformly to $b$ bits between its 1st and 99th training percentiles. This needs no labels, no shared basis and no coordination, but the bases of different parties are incompatible.

\paragraph{R2: few rounds of logits.}
Each party trains a small MLP from its frozen features to $C$ logits, and the server sums the parties' logits. At every step the parties send $C$ logits per sample and receive their gradients; the server never receives an embedding.

\paragraph{R3: split learning.}
Activations go up and gradients come down at every mini-batch. The bottoms are ViT-B backbones, frozen (only the top learns, on augmented images), adapted with LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora} (rank 16, attention query and value) or fully fine-tuned; ResNet-18 bottoms are the non-foundation references.

\paragraph{R4: a ridge head under encryption.}
After a local PCA and standardisation, the server fits $W=(X^\top X+\lambda I)^{-1}X^\top Y$. Each party encrypts its feature columns once with CKKS under a threshold key generated by the parties~\cite{mouchet2021multiparty}; the server computes the cross-party blocks $X_k^\top X_l$ and $X_k^\top Y$ homomorphically, masks them, has them decrypted collectively and solves in the clear. No per-sample vector is decrypted, but the server learns $X^\top X$ and $X^\top Y$, as in other closed-form protocols~\cite{gascon2017privacy,huang2026vafl}, and the cost grows as $\mathcal{O}(D^2)$ with $D=Kd'$. We emulate it in plaintext and time the homomorphic operations with a single key (App.~\ref{app:privacy}).

\paragraph{Cost accounting.}\phantomsection\label{sec:accounting}
Per aligned training sample, we count: (i)~\emph{bytes}, the cumulative payload of a training run over all parties and both directions, in half precision. R1 costs $Kd'b/8$ bytes once ($b=16$ unquantised), whatever the number of epochs, split learning $4Kd_{\mathrm{act}}$ bytes per epoch (half with frozen bottoms), and R2 $4KC$. (ii)~\emph{Sequential messages} until trained: one for R1, two per mini-batch for R2 and R3 (logits or activations up, gradients down; one with frozen bottoms), plus a validation pass per epoch, a few for R4. (iii)~\emph{Party compute}, in GFLOPs per training image, forward and backward; R1, R2 and R4 need one cacheable forward pass per view and no backpropagation through the encoder (R2 trains a small head per party). (iv)~\emph{Server view}: embeddings or PCA codes (R1), logits (R2), activations (R3), aggregates (R4).
