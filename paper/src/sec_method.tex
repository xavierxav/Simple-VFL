\section{One-Shot VFL with Frozen Foundation Features}\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Setting.}
$K$ parties $P_1,\dots,P_K$ hold different views $x_i^{(k)}$ of the same $N$ samples $i=1..N$, aligned by construction (no entity resolution is performed). The active party holds the labels $y_i$. Raw images, and the foundation model, never leave their owner.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
(1) Each party applies a \emph{frozen} pre-trained encoder $f$ locally and obtains $z_i^{(k)}=f(x_i^{(k)})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ (for DINOv2~\cite{oquab2024dinov2}, the class token, $d=1536$ for ViT-g/14). (2) Each party optionally compresses it, $\tilde z_i^{(k)}=Q(z_i^{(k)})$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:compress}), and sends it \emph{once} to the active party. (3) The active party trains a head $h_\theta$ on the concatenation $[\tilde z_i^{(1)};\dots;\tilde z_i^{(K)}]$ for as many epochs as it wants: these epochs are local, there is no gradient flow back to the parties, no party-side training, and no further communication. At inference, each party uploads $\tilde z^{(k)}$ for the new sample, again once. We use two heads: a linear layer, and a two-layer MLP ($K d\!\to\!1000\!\to\!C$, batch-norm, ReLU).

\paragraph{Communication accounting.}
We count payload bytes analytically (no network is simulated, no protocol overhead). The one-shot upload costs $K\,d\,b/8$ bytes per sample ($b$ bits per coordinate), e.g.\ $16\times1536\times4=96$\,KiB for 16 parties in float32, \emph{independently of the number of epochs}. Split learning exchanges, at every epoch, the activation of every bottom model forward and its gradient backward, i.e.\ $2\,K\,d_{\mathrm{act}}\,b/8$ bytes per sample and epoch.

\paragraph{Compression of the upload.}\label{sec:compress}
Because the encoder is frozen and identical for all samples, the representation can be compressed \emph{without} any collaboration between parties: each party fits a PCA on its own training features (keeping $d'\le d$ components) and uniformly quantises every coordinate to $b\in\{1,2,4,8\}$ bits between its 1st and 99th training percentiles (or casts it to 16-bit floats). The head is trained on the de-quantised values. The PCA and the quantiser statistics are computed on the training rows only.

\paragraph{Inversion attack and noise.}\label{sec:attack}
To measure leakage we assume a curious coordinator who also holds auxiliary image--feature pairs from the same distribution (the training pairs of the parties, a strong assumption), trains a convolutional decoder $g$ from the \emph{uploaded format} (compressed or noised) to a $64\!\times\!64$ RGB thumbnail with a pixel-wise MSE loss, and applies it to the uploads of held-out test samples. We report PSNR and SSIM of the reconstructions, with two reference points: the mean training image (no information) and nearest-neighbour retrieval from the training gallery. As a perturbation we add i.i.d.\ Gaussian noise whose standard deviation is $\rho$ times the per-coordinate training standard deviation. For an indicative differential-privacy reading we clip each upload to the median $\ell_2$ norm $c$ and use isotropic noise with the same average per-coordinate scale; replacing one record changes the clipped upload by at most $2c$ in $\ell_2$, so the release is $\mu$-Gaussian-DP with $\mu=2c/\sigma$ and the exact $(\varepsilon,\delta)$ follows from~\cite{dong2022gdp}. This is a \emph{per-upload local} guarantee for the release of one feature vector, not a guarantee for the trained model.
