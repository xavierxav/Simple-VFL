\section{One-Shot VFL with Frozen Foundation Features}\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Setting.}
$K$ parties $P_1,\dots,P_K$ hold different views $x_i^{(k)}$ of the same $N$ entities $i=1..N$ (a location, a lesion, an object), aligned beforehand, e.g.\ by private set intersection. The label owner holds $y_i$ and may also be one of the parties. Raw images never leave their owner; the encoder is public.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
(1) Each party applies a \emph{frozen} pre-trained encoder $f$ to its images and obtains $z_i^{(k)}=f(x_i^{(k)})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$, the pooled output token ($d=1536$ for DINOv2 ViT-g/14~\cite{oquab2024dinov2}, $1024$ for CLIP ViT-L/14~\cite{radford2021clip}); a party holding several images of an entity (revisits, views) averages their features. (2) Each party may compress its vectors without any coordination (below) and sends them \emph{once} to the label owner. (3) The label owner trains a head on the concatenation $[z_i^{(1)};\dots;z_i^{(K)}]$, for as many epochs as it wants, without gradients flowing back and without further communication. At inference, the parties upload the vector of the new entity, again once. Our reference head is a two-layer MLP ($Kd\!\to\!1000\!\to\!C$, batch normalisation, ReLU); Section~\ref{sec:exp-privacy} uses linear heads. Section~\ref{sec:results} also uses three variants that need nothing more from the parties: \emph{late fusion} trains one MLP per party and averages their log-probabilities with a weight $w$ (chosen on the validation fold, or fixed to $\tfrac12$); a \emph{branch} head projects each party to 512 dimensions (linear, LayerNorm, GELU), classifies their concatenation, adds one auxiliary classifier per party (loss weight $\tfrac12$) and, with modality dropout, zeroes one random party in 30\,\% of the training samples; a \emph{pooling} head normalises each party's vector (LayerNorm), averages them over the parties present, and applies the MLP, which only makes sense when all parties use the same encoder and feature space; a \emph{set} head receives all the vectors of a party that holds several images (e.g.\ 16 revisits), applies one weight-shared MLP to each of them and pools them by attention before the classifier.

\paragraph{Communication and compute accounting.}
We count payload bytes analytically. Features are stored in half precision, so the plain upload costs $2Kd$ bytes per entity, \emph{independently of the number of epochs}. Split learning exchanges, at every epoch, every bottom activation forward and its gradient backward, i.e.\ $4Kd_{\mathrm{act}}$ bytes per training sample and epoch ($d_{\mathrm{act}}=512$ for a ResNet-18). Party-side compute is the measured GPU time and the number of forward FLOPs per image (a training step costs about three forward passes). Downloading a public encoder and the few hundred bytes of quantiser bounds are not counted.

\paragraph{Compression without coordination.}
Because the encoder is frozen, each party can fit a PCA on its own training features (keeping $d'\le d$ components; the basis stays with the party) and quantise every coordinate uniformly to $b$ bits between its 1st and 99th training percentiles. The head is trained on the de-quantised values.

\paragraph{Linear heads and encrypted training.}
With frozen inputs, the head can be linear, and linear models are where privacy-preserving training is mature. After a local PCA to $d'$ components and standardisation, the label owner can fit (i)~a \emph{ridge} classifier $W=(X^\top X+\lambda I)^{-1}X^\top Y$ on one-hot targets, which only needs the Gram matrix $X^\top X$ and $X^\top Y$, or (ii)~a one-vs-rest \emph{logistic} regression trained by a fixed number of full-batch Nesterov steps with the degree-3 polynomial sigmoid of~\cite{kim2018logistic}. Both run on data encrypted with CKKS: for (i), every party encrypts its feature columns once under a threshold key generated jointly by the parties~\cite{mouchet2021multiparty} (a single key shared by the passive parties would let each of them decrypt the others' uploads); the label owner computes the cross-party blocks $X_k^\top X_l$ and $X_k^\top Y$ homomorphically (multiplicative depth one), masks them with random matrices, has them decrypted and solves the system in the clear---one upload plus one round trip of $\mathcal{O}(D^2)$ aggregates, $D=Kd'$, and no per-sample feature is ever decrypted. Class weights depend on the labels: the label owner then also sends the encrypted per-sample weights, every party multiplies its columns by them and uploads these weighted columns as well, and computes its own weighted Gram block with ciphertext--plaintext products (depth two). The regularisation $\lambda$ is chosen on validation predictions, which the label owner obtains by applying each candidate model to the encrypted validation uploads (ciphertext--plaintext products) and decrypting only the scores. The protocol reveals the second-order statistics of the features to the label owner, as the closed-form protocols of~\cite{gascon2017privacy,huang2026vafl}. Protocol (ii) reveals only the model, but needs bootstrapping or a refresh of the encrypted weights every few iterations~\cite{kim2018logistic,han2019logistic}. We evaluate (i), emulated in plaintext (CKKS adds errors of the order of $10^{-6}$ relative), and time its homomorphic operations with a single key; the interactive key generation and collective decryption of threshold CKKS are not timed. The accuracy of (ii) depends on its encrypted iteration budget and is not reported.

\paragraph{Inversion attack.}
To measure what the upload reveals, we assume a curious label owner who holds auxiliary image--feature pairs from the same distribution (cheap when the imagery and the encoder are public) and trains a decoder from the uploaded vector to a $64\!\times\!64$ RGB thumbnail with a pixel-wise MSE loss. The decoder starts with a learned affine map per party, initialised at the identity, so that it can undo party-specific PCA bases, followed by a shared deconvolutional network. The attack maps one uploaded vector to the image of its party. We report PSNR, SSIM~\cite{wang2004ssim} and a re-identification rate (the share of held-out test images whose reconstruction is closer to them than to any other test image), calibrated by the mean training image, the image's own mean colour and the nearest training image in feature space. Re-identification compares $16\times16$ thumbnails. For compressed uploads we assume the worst case of an attacker who knows the party's PCA basis and trains on uploads in that basis. A decoder trained with a pixel loss is a weak attacker: generative decoders recover more of the semantics of self-supervised and CLIP embeddings~\cite{bordes2022rcdm,ramesh2022unclip}, so our numbers are lower bounds on the leakage.

\paragraph{Differential privacy of the upload.}
We also evaluate the clipped Gaussian mechanism on the uploaded vectors: every per-party vector (raw or after PCA) is clipped to the median $\ell_2$ norm $c$ of the training vectors and released with noise $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 I)$ (the clipping norm is computed on the training data and is not itself privatised). Replacing one record changes a clipped vector by at most $2c$, so each release is $\mu$-Gaussian-DP with $\mu=2c/\sigma$~\cite{dong2022gdp}; we set $\sigma$ from a target per-upload $(\varepsilon,\delta=10^{-5})$ and measure the accuracy and the leakage of that same mechanism. The 16 uploads of a location compose to $4\mu$ (Gaussian DP composes as $\sqrt{16}\,\mu$): a per-upload $\varepsilon=8$ gives $\varepsilon\approx50$ for the location, and $\varepsilon=64$ gives $\varepsilon\approx630$ ($\delta=10^{-5}$). This is a local guarantee for the released vectors.
