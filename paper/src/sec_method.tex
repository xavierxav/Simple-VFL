\section{Operating Regimes and Cost Accounting}\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Setting and threat model.}
$K$ parties hold different images $x_i^{(k)}$ of the same $N$ entities $i$ (an area, a lesion), aligned beforehand, e.g.\ by private set intersection. The label owner, the \emph{server}, holds the labels and may also be a party. Raw images never leave their owner, and the encoder $f$ is public. The server is honest but curious: it follows the protocol and tries to recover the parties' images or to link uploads to images, knowing $f$, public images of the domain and, in the worst case, copies of the candidate images.

\paragraph{R1: one upload.}\phantomsection\label{sec:r1}
Each party computes $z_i^{(k)}=f(x_i^{(k)})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$, the pooled output token of a frozen encoder ($d=768$ to $1536$), averaged over the images it holds of an entity (revisits, or rotated copies of a photograph), and sends it \emph{once}. The server trains a head on the concatenation of the parties' vectors, for as many epochs as it wants: a two-layer MLP ($Kd\!\to\!1000\!\to\!C$), or on MILK10k \emph{late fusion}, which averages the log-probabilities of one MLP per party. \emph{Local compression:} each party fits a PCA on its own training features, keeps $d'$ components and quantises each one uniformly to $b$ bits between its 1st and 99th training percentiles. This needs no labels, no shared basis and no coordination, but the samples must still be aligned, and the bases of different parties are incompatible.

\paragraph{R2: few rounds of logits.}
Each party trains a small MLP from its frozen features to $C$ logits, and the server sums the parties' logits. At every step the parties send $C$ logits per sample and receive their gradients; the server never receives an embedding.

\paragraph{R3: split learning.}
Activations go up and gradients come down at every mini-batch. The bottoms are ViT-B backbones, frozen (only the top model learns, on augmented images), adapted with LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora} (rank 16 on the attention query and value projections) or fully fine-tuned; ResNet-18 bottoms trained from scratch or from ImageNet weights are the non-foundation references.

\paragraph{R4: a ridge head under encryption.}
After a local PCA and standardisation, the server fits $W=(X^\top X+\lambda I)^{-1}X^\top Y$. Each party encrypts its feature columns once with CKKS under a threshold key generated by the parties~\cite{mouchet2021multiparty}; the server computes the cross-party blocks $X_k^\top X_l$ and $X_k^\top Y$ homomorphically, masks them, has them decrypted collectively and solves in the clear. No per-sample vector is decrypted, but the server learns $X^\top X$ and $X^\top Y$, as in other closed-form protocols~\cite{gascon2017privacy,huang2026vafl}, and the cost grows as $\mathcal{O}(D^2)$ with $D=Kd'$. $\lambda$ is chosen from decrypted validation scores. We emulate the ridge in plaintext, measure the CKKS error, and time the homomorphic operations with a single key (details in App.~\ref{app:privacy}).

\paragraph{Cost accounting.}\phantomsection\label{sec:accounting}
Per aligned training sample, we count: (i)~\emph{bytes}, the cumulative payload of a training run over all parties and both directions, in half precision. R1 costs $Kd'b/8$ bytes once ($b=16$ unquantised), whatever the number of epochs or re-trainings, split learning $4Kd_{\mathrm{act}}$ bytes per epoch (half with frozen bottoms), and R2 $4KC$ bytes per epoch; inference and the encoder download are not counted. (ii)~\emph{Rounds}: one for R1; one per mini-batch for R2 and R3; one upload and one round trip of aggregates for R4. (iii)~\emph{Party compute}, in forward GFLOPs per image (a training step costs about three). In R1, R2 and R4 the parties run one forward pass per image, without backpropagation, and can cache the features. (iv)~\emph{Server view}: embeddings or PCA codes (R1), logits (R2), activations (R3), aggregates (R4).
