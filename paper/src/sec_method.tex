\section{One-Shot VFL with Frozen Foundation Features}\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Setting.}
$K$ parties $P_1,\dots,P_K$ hold different views $x_i^{(k)}$ of the same $N$ samples $i=1..N$, aligned by construction (no entity resolution is performed). The active party holds the labels $y_i$. Raw images never leave their owner; the encoder is public and identical for all parties.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
(1) Each party applies a \emph{frozen} pre-trained encoder $f$ locally and obtains $z_i^{(k)}=f(x_i^{(k)})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ (the pooled class token; $d=1536$ for DINOv2 ViT-g/14~\cite{oquab2024dinov2}, $1024$ for CLIP ViT-L/14~\cite{radford2021clip}). (2) Each party optionally compresses it, $\tilde z_i^{(k)}=Q(z_i^{(k)})$ (see below), and sends it \emph{once} to the active party. (3) The active party trains a head $h_\theta$ on the concatenation $[\tilde z_i^{(1)};\dots;\tilde z_i^{(K)}]$ for as many epochs as it wants: these epochs are local, there is no gradient flow back to the parties, no party-side training, and no further communication. At inference, each party uploads $\tilde z^{(k)}$ for the new sample, again once. We use two heads: a linear layer, and a two-layer MLP ($K d\!\to\!1000\!\to\!C$, batch-norm, ReLU).

\paragraph{Communication accounting.}
We count payload bytes analytically (no network is simulated, no protocol overhead). The features are computed in half precision, so the plain one-shot upload costs $K\,d\times2$ bytes per sample, e.g.\ $16\times1536\times2=48$\,KiB for DINOv2-g, \emph{independently of the number of epochs}. Split learning exchanges, at every epoch, the activation of every bottom model forward and its gradient backward, i.e.\ $2\times K\,d_{\mathrm{act}}\times2$ bytes per sample and epoch in half precision ($d_{\mathrm{act}}=512$ for a ResNet-18). One-time costs common to all protocols (downloading a public encoder, a few hundred bytes of quantiser bounds per party) are not counted.

\paragraph{Compression of the upload.}
Because the encoder is frozen and identical for all samples, the representation can be compressed \emph{without} any collaboration between parties: each party fits a PCA on its own training features (keeping $d'\le d$ components; the basis stays with the party) and uniformly quantises every coordinate to $b\in\{1,2,4,8\}$ bits between its 1st and 99th training percentiles; the $2d'$ bounds are sent once. The head is trained on the de-quantised values. The PCA and the quantiser statistics are computed on the training rows only.

\paragraph{Inversion attack.}
To measure leakage we assume a curious label owner who knows which party sent which vector and holds auxiliary image--feature pairs from the same distribution: here the training pairs of all parties, an assumption that is cheap to meet since the imagery and the encoder are public. It trains a decoder $g$ from the \emph{uploaded format} to a $64\!\times\!64$ RGB thumbnail with a pixel-wise MSE loss and applies it to the uploads of held-out test samples. The decoder starts with a learned affine map per party, initialised at the identity, so that it can undo the party-specific PCA bases, followed by a shared deconvolutional network. We consider a \emph{single-upload} attack (one vector $\to$ the image of its party) and a \emph{joint} attack (the 16 uploads of a location and a target party $\to$ the image of that party). We report PSNR and SSIM~\cite{wang2004ssim} and a re-identification rate: the fraction of test images whose reconstruction is closer to them than to any of the other 12.5k test images. Three references calibrate these numbers: the mean training image (no information), each image replaced by its own mean colour (an oracle that knows only the global colour), and the nearest training image in feature space.

\paragraph{Noise and differential privacy.}
We evaluate the clipped Gaussian mechanism directly: every per-party vector (raw or after PCA) is clipped to the median $\ell_2$ norm $c$ of the training vectors and released with isotropic noise $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 I)$. Replacing one record changes a clipped vector by at most $2c$, so each release is $\mu$-Gaussian-DP with $\mu=2c/\sigma$~\cite{dong2022gdp}; we set $\sigma$ from a target per-upload $(\varepsilon,\delta=10^{-5})$ and measure the accuracy and the leakage of that \emph{same} mechanism (noise drawn once, as in a release; the attacker re-draws it at every training step). The 16 uploads of a location compose to $\sqrt{16}\,\mu=4\mu$. This is a local guarantee for the released vectors, not for the model trained on top.
