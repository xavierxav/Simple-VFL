\section{One Upload, Its Alternatives and Their Costs}\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Setting and threat model.}
$K$ parties hold different images $x_i^{(k)}$ of the same $N$ entities $i$, aligned beforehand, e.g.\ by private set intersection. The label owner, the \emph{server}, holds the labels and may also be a party. Raw images never leave their owner, and the encoder $f$ is public. The server is honest but curious: it tries to recover the parties' images or link uploads to them, knowing $f$, public images of the domain and, at worst, copies of the candidates.

\paragraph{Our protocol: one upload of frozen features.}\phantomsection\label{sec:r1}
Each party computes $z_i^{(k)}=f(x_i^{(k)})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$, the pooled output vector of a frozen encoder ($d=384$ to $2048$), averaged over the images it holds of an entity (revisits, or rotated copies of a photograph), and sends it \emph{once}. The server then trains a head alone, for as many epochs as it wants: a two-layer MLP on the concatenated features, or, on MILK10k, \emph{late fusion}: one small MLP per party, each predicting the class from that party's features alone, whose log-probabilities the server averages. \emph{Local compression:} each party fits a PCA on its own training features, keeps $d'$ components and quantises each one uniformly to $b$ bits between its 1st and 99th training percentiles. This needs no labels, no shared basis and no coordination, but the bases of different parties are incompatible. Up to the encoder, this is FedOnce~\cite{wu2022fedonce}, whose parties instead train their encoders without labels.

\paragraph{Two privacy add-ons.}
\emph{DP noise:} each party clips its (compressed) vector and adds Gaussian noise before the upload (Sec.~\ref{sec:server}). \emph{Encrypted ridge:} after a local PCA and standardisation, the server fits a linear ridge classifier $W=(X^\top X+\lambda I)^{-1}X^\top Y$, where $X$ stacks the parties' features of the training samples side by side, $Y$ holds their one-hot labels and $\lambda$ is a regularisation weight. $W$ depends on the data only through two sums over the training samples: the feature covariances $X^\top X$ and the feature--label correlations $X^\top Y$. Each party encrypts its feature columns once with CKKS, a homomorphic encryption scheme for real vectors, under a key generated jointly by the parties, so that decryption needs all of them~\cite{mouchet2021multiparty}; the server computes the two sums on the ciphertexts, has them decrypted collectively and solves for $W$ in the clear. No individual vector is ever decrypted; the server learns the two sums, the validation scores used to choose $\lambda$ and the model, as in other closed-form protocols~\cite{gascon2017privacy,huang2026vafl}. This holds against an honest-but-curious server, unless every party colludes with it; the decrypted sums are aggregates, not formally private. The cost grows as $\mathcal{O}(D^2)$ with $D=Kd'$. We emulate the ridge in plaintext and time the homomorphic operations with a single key (App.~\ref{app:privacy}).

\paragraph{Interactive alternatives.}
\emph{Logit heads:} each party trains a small MLP from its frozen features to the $C$ class logits, and the server adds the parties' logits. At every mini-batch the parties send $C$ logits per sample and receive their gradients: the server never receives an embedding, but training takes hundreds of sequential messages. \emph{Split learning:} activations go up and gradients come down at every mini-batch. The bottoms are ViT-B foundation backbones, frozen (only the top learns, on augmented images), adapted with LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora} (rank 16, attention query and value) or fully fine-tuned; ResNet-18 bottoms~\cite{he2016resnet}, trained from random initialisation or from ImageNet weights, are the non-foundation references.

\paragraph{Cost accounting.}\phantomsection\label{sec:accounting}
Per aligned training sample, we count: (i)~\emph{bytes}, the cumulative payload of a training run over all parties and both directions, in half precision. One upload costs $Kd'b/8$ bytes ($b=16$ unquantised), whatever the number of epochs; split learning $4Kd_{\mathrm{act}}$ bytes per epoch, with $d_{\mathrm{act}}$ the size of a party's activation (half with frozen bottoms), and logit heads $4KC$ per epoch. (ii)~\emph{Sequential messages} until trained: one for an upload, since all parties upload in parallel; two per mini-batch for logit heads and split learning (one with frozen bottoms), plus a validation pass per epoch; an upload and a decryption round trip for the encrypted ridge. (iii)~\emph{Party compute}, in GFLOPs per training image, forward and backward: one upload, logit heads and the encrypted ridge need one forward pass per view, which can be cached, and no backpropagation through the encoder. (iv)~\emph{What the server sees}: features or PCA codes (one upload), logits (logit heads), activations (split learning) or two decrypted sums (encrypted ridge).
