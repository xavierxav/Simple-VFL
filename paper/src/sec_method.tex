\section{One-Shot VFL with Frozen Foundation Features}\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Setting.}
$K$ parties $P_1,\dots,P_K$ hold different views $x_i^{(k)}$ of the same $N$ entities $i=1..N$ (a location, a lesion, an object), aligned beforehand, e.g.\ by private set intersection. The label owner holds $y_i$ and may also be one of the parties. Raw images never leave their owner; the encoder is public.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
(1) Each party applies a \emph{frozen} pre-trained encoder $f$ to its images and obtains $z_i^{(k)}=f(x_i^{(k)})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$, the pooled output token ($d=1536$ for DINOv2 ViT-g/14~\cite{oquab2024dinov2}, $1024$ for CLIP ViT-L/14~\cite{radford2021clip}); a party holding several images of an entity (revisits, views) averages their features. (2) Each party may compress its vectors without any coordination (below) and sends them \emph{once} to the label owner. (3) The label owner trains a head on the concatenation $[z_i^{(1)};\dots;z_i^{(K)}]$, for as many epochs as it wants, without gradients flowing back and without further communication. At inference, the parties upload the vector of the new entity, again once. Our reference head is a two-layer MLP ($Kd\!\to\!1000\!\to\!C$, batch normalisation, ReLU); Section~\ref{sec:exp-privacy} uses linear heads.

\paragraph{Communication and compute accounting.}
We count payload bytes analytically. Features are stored in half precision, so the plain upload costs $2Kd$ bytes per entity, \emph{independently of the number of epochs}. Split learning exchanges, at every epoch, every bottom activation forward and its gradient backward, i.e.\ $4Kd_{\mathrm{act}}$ bytes per training sample and epoch ($d_{\mathrm{act}}=512$ for a ResNet-18). Party-side compute is the measured GPU time and the number of forward FLOPs per image (a training step costs about three forward passes). Downloading a public encoder and the few hundred bytes of quantiser bounds are not counted.

\paragraph{Compression without coordination.}
Because the encoder is frozen, each party can fit a PCA on its own training features (keeping $d'\le d$ components; the basis stays with the party) and quantise every coordinate uniformly to $b$ bits between its 1st and 99th training percentiles. The head is trained on the de-quantised values.

\paragraph{Linear heads and encrypted training.}
With frozen inputs, the head can be linear, and linear models are where privacy-preserving training is mature. After a local PCA to $d'$ components and standardisation, the label owner can fit (i)~a \emph{ridge} classifier $W=(X^\top X+\lambda I)^{-1}X^\top Y$ on one-hot targets, which only needs the Gram matrix $X^\top X$ and $X^\top Y$, or (ii)~a one-vs-rest \emph{logistic} regression trained by a fixed number of full-batch Nesterov steps with the degree-3 polynomial sigmoid of~\cite{kim2018logistic}. Both run on data encrypted with CKKS: for (i), every party encrypts its feature columns once under a key held by the passive parties (or a threshold key); the label owner computes the cross-party blocks $X_k^\top X_l$ and $X_k^\top Y$ homomorphically (multiplicative depth one, two with class weights), masks them with random matrices, has them decrypted and solves the system in the clear---one upload plus one round trip of $\mathcal{O}(D^2)$ aggregates, $D=Kd'$, and no per-sample feature is ever decrypted; it reveals the second-order statistics of the features to the label owner, as the closed-form protocols of~\cite{gascon2017privacy,huang2026vafl}. Protocol (ii) reveals only the model, but needs bootstrapping or a refresh of the encrypted weights every few iterations~\cite{kim2018logistic,han2019logistic}. We emulate both in plaintext (CKKS adds errors of the order of $10^{-6}$ relative) to measure their accuracy, and we time the homomorphic operations of (i).

\paragraph{Inversion attack.}
To measure what the upload reveals, we assume a curious label owner who holds auxiliary image--feature pairs from the same distribution (cheap when the imagery and the encoder are public) and trains a decoder from the uploaded vector to a $64\!\times\!64$ RGB thumbnail with a pixel-wise MSE loss. The decoder starts with a learned affine map per party, initialised at the identity, so that it can undo party-specific PCA bases, followed by a shared deconvolutional network. A \emph{single-upload} attack maps one vector to the image of its party; a \emph{joint} attack sees the 16 uploads of a location. We report PSNR, SSIM~\cite{wang2004ssim} and a re-identification rate (the share of held-out test images whose reconstruction is closer to them than to any other test image), calibrated by the mean training image, the image's own mean colour and the nearest training image in feature space.

\paragraph{Differential privacy of the upload.}
We also evaluate the clipped Gaussian mechanism on the uploaded vectors: every per-party vector (raw or after PCA) is clipped to the median $\ell_2$ norm $c$ of the training vectors and released with noise $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 I)$. Replacing one record changes a clipped vector by at most $2c$, so each release is $\mu$-Gaussian-DP with $\mu=2c/\sigma$~\cite{dong2022gdp}; we set $\sigma$ from a target per-upload $(\varepsilon,\delta=10^{-5})$ and measure the accuracy and the leakage of that same mechanism. The 16 uploads of a location compose to $4\mu$. This is a local guarantee for the released vectors.
