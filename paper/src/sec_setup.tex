\section{Natural Benchmarks for Image VFL}\label{sec:setup}

Our second contribution is a set of benchmarks whose parties see the same entity through different sensors, dates or devices, with an evaluation protocol (Table~\ref{tab:bench}). Split benchmarks instead cut one image: on MILK10k, cutting the dermoscopy into halves \emph{loses} information (\milkGHalves{} against \milkGDerm\,\% macro F1 for the whole image, DINOv2-g).

\paragraph{VertiBench Satellite, reproduced and audited.}\phantomsection\label{sec:vb-audit}
Each of the 16 parties holds one Sentinel-2 L1C revisit ($158\times158$ pixels, 13 bands) of 3{,}927 areas, labelled by their sampling source (majority class: \majVB\,\%). We keep the official split (3{,}142 training and \giVbNTest{} test samples). The parties are largely redundant: one gives \soloMean\,\% accuracy on average, all 16 give \partiesKAll\,\%. Our re-implementation of the published split-learning reference reaches $\reproAcc\pm\reproStd$\,\%, in line with the published value (Table~\ref{tab:main}). Image fingerprints map training and test samples to distinct source areas, none in both splits, but \auSibN{} test samples (\auSibShare\,\%) have a training tile of the same site, all in the two classes whose sites are cut into $3\times3$ tiles: sibling and class effects are confounded. SplitNN is more accurate on these samples (\auSnSib{} vs.\ \auSnNoSib\,\%), the CLIP-L one-shot head is not (\auClipSib{} vs.\ \auClipNoSib\,\%); we also report the subset without siblings (App.~\ref{app:audit}).

\paragraph{MILK10k: two photographs of the same lesion.}
MILK10k~\cite{milk10k} pairs, for \milkN{} lesions, a clinical close-up (party~A, e.g.\ the referring practitioner) and a dermoscopic image (party~B, the dermatologist), with metadata (optional party~C) and a confirmed diagnosis (7 classes). Without patient identifiers, the five stratified folds are drawn over lesions; with 40 to 61 lesions of the two rarest classes per test fold, macro F1 is the metric. The two parties are wrong together on \mfGBoth\,\% of the lesions, against \mfGIndep\,\% if their errors were independent (App.~\ref{app:milk}).

\paragraph{WorldStrat: two sensors.}
From WorldStrat~\cite{cornebise2022worldstrat} we build a two-party task on \wsIpccN{} areas: a 1.5\,m SPOT image (party~A) and 16 Sentinel-2 revisits (party~B), labelled at the centre of the area by land cover~\cite{esa2017cci} (18 classes), its IPCC category (7) or the degree of urbanisation~\cite{pesaresi2019ghsl} (8). Stratified folds put neighbouring tiles of a site in training and test and overstate macro F1 by about 6 to 9 points on land cover, so folds are grouped by site. Adding the Sentinel-2 party to SPOT then changes macro F1 by \wgIpccA{} points on IPCC (\wgIpccAW{} folds improve), \wgLccsA{} on land cover (\wgLccsAW) and \wgSmodA{} on urbanisation (\wgSmodAW): the second sensor adds little, so this benchmark tests fusion rather than the frontier (App.~\ref{app:ws}).

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Costs follow Sec.~\ref{sec:accounting}. \emph{Baselines} on raw images: the reproduced VertiBench SplitNN (one ResNet-18 per party, 13 bands, 50 epochs); our split-learning recipe with ResNet-18 bottoms~\cite{he2016resnet} from scratch or ImageNet weights; FedOnce~\cite{wu2022fedonce} with Noise As Targets~\cite{bojanowski2017nat} or SimCLR~\cite{chen2020simclr}; and R3 with ViT-B bottoms (10 epochs, augmentation). None was tuned extensively. \emph{Encoders} take 224\,px RGB input (list in App.~\ref{app:vb}). CLIP ViT-L/14 is the main VertiBench encoder (most accurate alone, with a public generative decoder); DINOv2 ViT-g/14, with the largest $d$, serves for compression, missing parties and the MILK10k reference; adapted backbones are ViT-B, always compared with the same ViT-B frozen. \emph{Statistics:} on VertiBench, mean\,$\pm$\,std over five seeds on the fixed test set (three for ViT split learning) and paired comparisons with the reproduced SplitNN on the same test predictions (exact McNemar test, paired bootstrap); on MILK10k and WorldStrat, five folds, paired by fold.
