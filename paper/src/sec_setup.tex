\section{Benchmarks and Evaluation Protocol}\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{VertiBench Satellite.}
The Satellite data set of VertiBench~\cite{wu2024vertibench} gives, for 3{,}927 WorldStrat~\cite{cornebise2022worldstrat} areas, 16 Sentinel-2 L1C revisits (13 bands, $158\times158$ pixels, 8 bits) to $K=16$ parties, and a four-class label (the provider of the area; majority class 59.9\,\% of the test set). We use the public split as is (3{,}142 training and 785 test samples); 15\,\% of the training samples are held out for validation with the seed, so seeds change the initialisation and the validation subset, not the test set. The 16 parties observe the same sensor at different dates.

\paragraph{WorldStrat, two sensors.}
To obtain parties with genuinely different sensors we build, from the WorldStrat release, a two-party task for its 3{,}925 usable areas of $1.58\times1.58$\,km: party A holds the SPOT 6/7 pan-sharpened image (RGB, 1.5\,m, $1050\times1050$ pixels), party B the 16 Sentinel-2 L1C revisits (13 bands, 10\,m). The label owner holds one of three labels of the WorldStrat metadata, all read at the centre of the area: the ESA-CCI LCCS land-cover class of the 300\,m pixel of the 2020 map~\cite{esa2017cci} (the 18 classes with at least 40 areas), its coarser IPCC category (7 classes), and the GHSL degree of urbanisation of the 1\,km SMOD cell (8 classes from rural very-low-density to urban centre~\cite{pesaresi2019ghsl}). A label therefore describes only the centre of the image (4\,\% of its area for LCCS and IPCC); besides the full images, the parties also encode centre crops of 336\,m and 1\,km. To vary the information held by party A, its image is degraded to 3, 6 and 10\,m before encoding; it can also encode 16 tiles of $262$\,px at full resolution and upload their mean. Party B encodes every revisit as three composites (RGB, near and short-wave infrared, below) and uploads, per composite, the mean over the 16 revisits or over the 8 least cloudy ones (ranked by a cloud score computed locally), possibly with the temporal standard deviation; for a set head it uploads all 16 vectors.

\paragraph{MILK10k.}
MILK10k~\cite{milk10k} pairs, for 5{,}240 skin lesions, a clinical close-up photograph and a dermoscopic image, with the patient's age, sex, anatomical site and skin tone (party C: standardised age with a missing-value indicator and one-hot categories), and a diagnosis confirmed by histopathology or experts. Party A (e.g.\ the referring practitioner) holds the clinical photograph, party B (the dermatologist) the dermoscopy, and an optional party C the metadata; the label owner holds the diagnosis. We group the 11 diagnoses into 7 classes (BCC, nevus, benign keratosis, SCC/keratoacanthoma, melanoma, actinic keratosis/intra-epidermal carcinoma, and the five rare classes as ``other''; BCC is 48\,\% of the lesions). The release has no patient identifiers, so folds are drawn over lesions. Large paired clinical--dermoscopic data were not available before this 2025 release (Derm7pt~\cite{kawahara2019derm7pt} has about 1{,}000 cases).

\paragraph{Classic split benchmarks.}
CIFAR-10/100~\cite{krizhevsky2009cifar} are split as in the papers we compare with: top/bottom halves ($K=2$) and $16\times16$ quadrants ($K=4$) as in VFLAIR~\cite{zou2024vflair} and FedOnce~\cite{wu2022fedonce}, and left/right halves with only $N_o\in\{256,\dots,2048\}$ aligned labelled samples as in Sun et al.~\cite{sun2023oneshotvfl}; halves are upsampled by 7 ($16\times32\to112\times224$) and quadrants by 14 ($16\times16\to224\times224$), so that four quadrants are encoded with twice as many tokens as two halves. ModelNet40~\cite{wu2015modelnet} uses 12 rendered views per object~\cite{su2015mvcnn} (9{,}843 training and 2{,}468 test objects), one view per party ($K=12$) or three views per party ($K=4$).

\paragraph{Frozen encoders.}
All encoders are used through their pooled output token at their native 224\,px input: DINOv2 ViT-S/B/L/g~\cite{oquab2024dinov2}, CLIP ViT-B/16 and ViT-L/14~\cite{radford2021clip}, SigLIP ViT-B/16~\cite{zhai2023siglip}, a supervised ImageNet ResNet-50 and a randomly initialised ViT-B as references; the Sentinel-2 model SSL4EO-S12 ViT-S/16~\cite{wang2023ssl4eo} on all 13 bands; and, for MILK10k, BiomedCLIP~\cite{zhang2023biomedclip} and the dermatology model MONET~\cite{kim2024monet}. Satellite images are standardised per band with statistics of the released data and fed as RGB, or as false-colour composites (B8-B4-B3, B12-B8A-B4) to expose the infrared bands to RGB encoders; the SPOT image is encoded at its own pixel count (1050, 518, 266 and 168\,px for 1.5, 3, 6 and 10\,m). Natural images use each model's own normalisation. Features are extracted once in half precision.

\paragraph{Baselines trained on the raw images.}
\emph{VertiBench recipe}: our re-implementation of the published SplitNN configuration (one ResNet-18 per party with a $9\times9$ stride-2 input convolution, MLP aggregation, Adam with learning rate $10^{-5}$, step decay, batch 32, 50 epochs, last epoch tested) on the 13 bands. \emph{SplitNN, our recipe}: standard ResNet-18 bottoms~\cite{he2016resnet}, from scratch or ImageNet weights, and the same MLP top model as our head (Adam, one-cycle schedule, 40 epochs, batch 64, flips, best validation checkpoint). \emph{FedOnce}~\cite{wu2022fedonce}: every party trains a ResNet-18 on its unlabelled training images with Noise As Targets~\cite{bojanowski2017nat} (64-d targets, Hungarian re-assignment every 3 epochs, 100 epochs) and uploads its output once; we also report a SimCLR~\cite{chen2020simclr} variant. \emph{Same-backbone split learning} (MILK10k): bottoms initialised from the frozen encoders' family (DINOv2 ViT-B/14, CLIP ViT-B/16) adapted with LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora} (rank 16 on the attention query and value projections) or fully fine-tuned, and ResNet-18 from scratch or ImageNet, all with augmentation, class-balanced loss and the best validation checkpoint. On the classic benchmarks we quote the published numbers of the original papers.

\paragraph{Training protocol and statistics.}
All heads on frozen features use Adam (learning rate $10^{-3}$, batch 512), at most 50 epochs, \texttt{ReduceLROnPlateau} and early stopping on the (unweighted) validation loss; the best validation checkpoint is tested (Table~\ref{tab:milkreg} checks two other stopping rules). These settings are shared by all encoders and benchmarks. On the imbalanced WorldStrat and MILK10k labels the loss is class-balanced and macro F1 is the primary metric. We report mean $\pm$ sample standard deviation over five seeds (fixed VertiBench test set), three seeds (CIFAR, ModelNet; five for the protocol of Sun et al.) or five stratified cross-validation folds (WorldStrat, MILK10k; fold $f$ is the test set, fold $f{+}1$ the validation set). On the fixed VertiBench test set, a single accuracy around 80\,\% has a binomial 95\,\% interval of $\pm2.8$ points; the comparison of the two best encoders is paired (bootstrap and exact McNemar test on the same test predictions), and MILK10k comparisons are paired by fold.
