\section{Natural Benchmarks for Image VFL}\label{sec:setup}

Our second contribution is a set of benchmarks whose parties see the same entity through different sensors, dates or devices, with an evaluation protocol (Table~\ref{tab:bench}). Split benchmarks instead cut one image: on MILK10k, cutting the dermoscopy into halves \emph{loses} information (\milkGHalves{} against \milkGDerm\,\% macro F1 for the whole image, DINOv2-g).

\paragraph{VertiBench Satellite, reproduced and audited.}\phantomsection\label{sec:vb-audit}
Each of the 16 parties holds one Sentinel-2 L1C revisit of 3{,}927 areas, labelled by their sampling source (majority class: \majVB\,\%). We keep the official split (3{,}142 training and \giVbNTest{} test samples). The parties are largely redundant: one gives \soloMean\,\% accuracy on average, all 16 give \partiesKAll\,\%. Our re-implementation of the published split-learning reference reaches $\reproAcc\pm\reproStd$\,\%, in line with the published value (Table~\ref{tab:main}). No matched source area is in both splits, but \auSibN{} test samples (\auSibShare\,\%) have a training tile of the same site, all in the two classes whose sites are cut into $3\times3$ tiles: sibling and class effects are confounded. SplitNN is more accurate on these samples (\auSnSib{} vs.\ \auSnNoSib\,\%), the CLIP-L one-shot head is not (\auClipSib{} vs.\ \auClipNoSib\,\%); we also report the subset without siblings (App.~\ref{app:audit}).

\paragraph{MILK10k: two photographs of the same lesion.}
MILK10k~\cite{milk10k} pairs, for \milkN{} lesions, a clinical close-up (party~A) and a dermoscopic image (party~B), with metadata (optional party~C) and a confirmed diagnosis (7 classes). Without patient identifiers, the five stratified folds are drawn over lesions; with 40 to 61 lesions of the two rarest classes per test fold, macro F1 is the metric. The two parties are wrong together on \mfGBoth\,\% of the lesions, against \mfGIndep\,\% if their errors were independent (App.~\ref{app:milk}).

\paragraph{WorldStrat: two sensors.}
Our two-party task pairs, on \wsIpccN{} WorldStrat~\cite{cornebise2022worldstrat} areas (\wsLccsN{} for land cover), a SPOT image (party~A) with 16 Sentinel-2 revisits (party~B), labelled by land cover~\cite{esa2017cci}, IPCC category or degree of urbanisation~\cite{pesaresi2019ghsl}. Stratified folds put neighbouring tiles of a site in training and test and overstate macro F1 by about 6 to 9 points on land cover, so folds are grouped by site. Fusing the two sensors then reaches \wsIpccFus, \wsLccsFus{} and \wsSmodFus\,\% macro F1 on IPCC, land cover and urbanisation, against \wsIpccSms, \wsLccsSms{} and \wsSmodSpot\,\% for the better sensor alone in the same configuration: the second sensor adds little, so this benchmark tests fusion rather than the frontier (App.~\ref{app:ws}). VertiBench Satellite is built from the same WorldStrat areas, so the two satellite benchmarks are not independent replications.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
\emph{Baselines} on raw images: the reproduced VertiBench SplitNN (one ResNet-18 per party, 13 bands, 50 epochs); our split-learning recipe with ResNet-18 bottoms~\cite{he2016resnet} from scratch or ImageNet weights; FedOnce~\cite{wu2022fedonce} with Noise As Targets~\cite{bojanowski2017nat} or SimCLR~\cite{chen2020simclr}; and R3 with ViT-B bottoms (10 epochs, augmentation). None was tuned extensively. \emph{Encoders}~\cite{oquab2024dinov2,radford2021clip,zhai2023siglip,wang2023ssl4eo,zhang2023biomedclip,kim2024monet} take 224\,px RGB input, 13 bands for SSL4EO-S12 (list in App.~\ref{app:vb}). CLIP ViT-L/14 is the main VertiBench encoder (most accurate alone, with a public decoder); DINOv2 ViT-g/14 serves for compression, missing parties and MILK10k; adapted ViT-B backbones are compared with the same ViT-B frozen. \emph{Statistics:} on VertiBench, mean\,$\pm$\,std over five seeds on the fixed test set (three for ViT split learning, the ImageNet-initialised recipe and the SplitNN re-run) and paired comparisons of three-seed ensembles with the reproduced SplitNN (exact McNemar test, paired bootstrap, Holm correction); on MILK10k and WorldStrat, five folds, paired by fold.
