\section{Natural Benchmarks for Image VFL}\label{sec:setup}

Our second contribution is a set of image VFL benchmarks whose parties are naturally distinct, together with an evaluation protocol (Table~\ref{tab:bench}). Classic image VFL benchmarks cut one image into halves or quadrants (CIFAR) or render views of an object (ModelNet~\cite{wu2015modelnet,su2015mvcnn}). On these, the pieces complement each other by construction, and frozen DINOv2 features exceed every published VFL number we compare with by a margin that mostly measures pre-training (App.~\ref{app:classic}). On MILK10k, the same practice of splitting one image into halves \emph{loses} information: the two halves of the dermoscopic image give \milkGHalves\,\% macro F1 with DINOv2-g against \milkGDerm\,\% for the whole image, because each party's encoder sees half a lesion. Natural parties instead see the same entity almost completely, through different sensors, dates or devices.

\paragraph{VertiBench Satellite, reproduced and audited.}\phantomsection\label{sec:vb-audit}
VertiBench~\cite{wu2024vertibench} gives, for 3{,}927 WorldStrat areas, 16 Sentinel-2 L1C revisits ($158\times158$ pixels, 13 bands) to 16 parties, and a four-class label, the sampling source of the area (majority class: \majVB\,\% of the test set). We keep the official split (3{,}142 training and \giVbNTest{} test samples). 15\,\% of the training samples are held out for validation with the seed, so seeds change the initialisation and the validation subset, not the test set. The parties observe the same sensor at different dates and are largely redundant: one party alone gives \soloMean\,\% accuracy on average, all 16 give \partiesKAll\,\% (App.~\ref{app:vb}). \emph{Reproduction:} our re-implementation of the published split-learning configuration reaches $\reproAcc\pm\reproStd$\,\%, in line with the published value (Table~\ref{tab:main}). \emph{Audit:} image fingerprints map \auMapTrain{} training and \auMapTest{} test samples to distinct source areas, and no area appears in both splits. However, \auSibN{} test samples (\auSibShare\,\%) have a training tile from the same site, all of them in the two classes whose sites are cut into $3\times3$ tiles, so sibling and class effects are confounded. The reproduced SplitNN is more accurate on these samples (\auSnSib{} vs.\ \auSnNoSib\,\% without a sibling), the CLIP-L one-shot head is not (\auClipSib{} vs.\ \auClipNoSib\,\%), and the margins of Sec.~\ref{sec:frontier-vb} are therefore larger on the subset without siblings (App.~\ref{app:audit}). We keep the official split for comparability and report both readings.

\paragraph{MILK10k: a natural two-party medical benchmark.}
MILK10k~\cite{milk10k} pairs, for \milkN{} skin lesions, a clinical close-up photograph and a dermoscopic image, with the patient's age, sex, anatomical site and skin tone, and a diagnosis confirmed by histopathology or by experts. Party~A (e.g.\ the referring practitioner) holds the clinical photograph, party~B (the dermatologist) the dermoscopy, an optional party~C the metadata, and the label owner the diagnosis. We group the 11 diagnoses into 7 classes, with the five rarest as ``other''; basal cell carcinoma is 48\,\% of the lesions. The release has no patient identifiers, so the five stratified folds are drawn over lesions and do not measure generalisation to new patients. Each test fold holds only 40 to 61 lesions of the two rarest classes, hence macro F1 as the primary metric and paired comparisons by fold. The dermoscopy alone gives \milkGDerm\,\% macro F1 with DINOv2-g, the clinical photograph \milkGClin\,\%, and their concatenation \milkGBoth\,\%: the two parties are wrong together on \mfGBoth\,\% of the lesions, against \mfGIndep\,\% if their errors were independent (App.~\ref{app:milk}).

\paragraph{WorldStrat: a natural two-sensor benchmark.}
From the WorldStrat release~\cite{cornebise2022worldstrat} we build a two-party task on \wsIpccN{} areas of $1.58\times1.58$\,km. Party~A holds a pan-sharpened SPOT image (RGB, 1.5\,m), party~B the 16 Sentinel-2 L1C revisits (13 bands, 10\,m), and the label owner one of three labels read at the centre of the area: the land-cover class of a 300\,m global map~\cite{esa2017cci} (18 classes), its coarser IPCC category (7 classes), and the degree of urbanisation~\cite{pesaresi2019ghsl} (8 classes). Neighbouring tiles of a site must stay in the same fold: stratified folds split them between training and test and overstate macro F1 by about 6 to 9 points on the two land-cover labels. With site-grouped folds, adding the Sentinel-2 party to the SPOT party changes macro F1 by \wgIpccA{} points on IPCC (\wgIpccAW{} folds improve), \wgLccsA{} on land cover (\wgLccsAW) and \wgSmodA{} on urbanisation (\wgSmodAW): the second sensor adds little, because both parties are wrong on the same areas (App.~\ref{app:ws}). The benchmark therefore tests fusion more than the frontier, and we do not use it in Sec.~\ref{sec:frontier}.

\paragraph{Evaluation protocol.}
\emph{Costs} are counted as in Sec.~\ref{sec:accounting}. \emph{Baselines} trained on the raw images are: the reproduced VertiBench SplitNN (one ResNet-18 per party with a $9\times9$ stride-2 input convolution, 13 bands, Adam with learning rate $10^{-5}$, batch 32, 50 epochs, last epoch tested); our own split-learning recipe with standard ResNet-18 bottoms~\cite{he2016resnet}, from scratch or ImageNet weights; FedOnce~\cite{wu2022fedonce}, whose parties pre-train a ResNet-18 on their unlabelled images with Noise As Targets~\cite{bojanowski2017nat} (or SimCLR~\cite{chen2020simclr}) and upload its output once; and same-backbone split learning (R3) with frozen, LoRA-adapted or fine-tuned ViT-B bottoms, trained for 10 epochs with augmentation and the best validation checkpoint. None was tuned extensively. \emph{Encoders} are used through their pooled output token at 224\,px: DINOv2 ViT-S/B/L/g~\cite{oquab2024dinov2}, CLIP ViT-B/16 and ViT-L/14~\cite{radford2021clip}, SigLIP ViT-B/16~\cite{zhai2023siglip}, a supervised ImageNet ResNet-50 and a randomly initialised ViT-B, the Sentinel-2 model SSL4EO-S12~\cite{wang2023ssl4eo} on 13 bands, and the medical encoders BiomedCLIP~\cite{zhang2023biomedclip} and MONET~\cite{kim2024monet} (App.~\ref{app:vb}). Satellite images are standardised per band and fed as RGB. \emph{Which encoder where:} CLIP ViT-L/14 is the main encoder on VertiBench, as the most accurate single encoder and the only one with a public generative decoder (Sec.~\ref{sec:server}); DINOv2 ViT-g/14, with the largest $d$, is used for the compression grid, the missing-party study and the MILK10k one-shot reference; and every adapted backbone is a ViT-B (CLIP ViT-B/16, DINOv2 ViT-B/14), compared with the same ViT-B frozen. \emph{Heads} on frozen features use Adam (learning rate $10^{-3}$, batch 512), at most 50 epochs and early stopping on the validation loss, with a class-balanced loss on MILK10k and WorldStrat. \emph{Statistics:} on VertiBench we report mean\,$\pm$\,sample standard deviation over five seeds on the fixed test set (three for ViT split learning), and compare methods with the reproduced SplitNN on the same test predictions (probabilities averaged over three seeds; exact McNemar test and paired bootstrap intervals with 10{,}000 resamples). On MILK10k and WorldStrat we report five folds (fold $f$ tests, fold $f{+}1$ validates) and pair comparisons by fold.
