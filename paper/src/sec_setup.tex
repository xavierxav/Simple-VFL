\section{Benchmarks and Evaluation Protocol}\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{VertiBench Satellite.}
The Satellite dataset of VertiBench~\cite{wu2024vertibench} contains, for about 3.9k WorldStrat~\cite{cornebise2022worldstrat} locations, 16 Sentinel-2 revisits (13 bands, $158\times158$ pixels, rescaled to 8 bits) that are distributed over $K=16$ parties, and a four-class label (the provider of the location: Amnesty, ASMSpotter, Landcover, UNHCR). We use the public train/test files as is: 3{,}142 training and 785 official test samples; 15\,\% of the training samples are held out for validation with the seed, and the \emph{test set is used once per run}. The published reference is a ResNet SplitNN trained end-to-end on all 13 bands: $81.17\pm0.35$\,\% accuracy. These 16 parties observe the same sensor at different dates; this is multi-view fusion, not a cross-silo split over different feature spaces, and the provider label is spatially clustered (see below).

\paragraph{WorldStrat, sensor-level split.}
To obtain parties with genuinely different data, we also use the SPOT (1.5\,m) and Sentinel-2 (10\,m, 16 revisits) images of the 3{,}925 usable WorldStrat locations: one party holds SPOT, a second party holds the Sentinel revisits (or sixteen parties hold one revisit each). We use three labels: the provider (\texttt{sources}, 4 classes), the IPCC land cover (7 classes, strongly imbalanced) and a 4-class simplification of it. All backbones are frozen DINOv2 ViT-g/14 class tokens computed from RGB bands. Two protocols are compared: the \emph{random} POI-grouped split of the preliminary version (2{,}668/668/589, five seeds), and a \emph{spatial} split in which the POIs are clustered by $k$-means on their 3D unit-sphere coordinates ($k{=}25$), whole clusters are assigned to five balanced folds, and each fold is in turn the test set (the next fold is the validation set), so that no test location has a training neighbour in its own cluster. A location-only random forest ($500$ trees on longitude/latitude) is evaluated under the same protocols to expose geographic shortcuts.

\paragraph{Baselines trained on the raw images.}
Both baselines use the full-resolution raw images ($158\times158$ pixels), either all 13 bands (as the published reference) or the three RGB bands seen by the frozen encoders. Each party's bottom model is a standard ResNet-18~\cite{he2016resnet} (ImageNet stem, only the number of input channels changed) trained from scratch. \emph{SplitNN}: the 16 bottom models and an MLP top model identical to our head are trained end-to-end for 40 epochs (Adam, one-cycle schedule with peak learning rate $2\cdot10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, batch 64, random flips, mixed precision); the checkpoint with the lowest validation loss is tested. \emph{FedOnce-style one-shot}~\cite{wu2022fedonce}: each party pre-trains its own ResNet-18 with SimCLR~\cite{chen2020simclr} (NT-Xent loss, temperature 0.2, two random resized $128\times128$ crops with flips, rotations, per-band gain/offset jitter and noise; 100 epochs, batch 256) on its unlabeled \emph{training} images only, uploads the 512-d embedding of each whole image once, and the active party trains the same head as for the frozen features. Wall-clock times are measured on one NVIDIA A100-40GB GPU (the frozen encoders also on an L4).

\paragraph{Training protocol and statistics.}
All heads use Adam (lr $10^{-3}$, batch 512), at most 50 epochs, \texttt{ReduceLROnPlateau} (factor 0.3, patience 4) and early stopping on the validation loss (patience 10), with the checkpoint of the best validation loss evaluated on the test set. We report accuracy and macro one-vs-rest AUC as mean $\pm$ standard deviation over five seeds (population standard deviation, $\texttt{ddof}{=}0$); seeds change the head initialisation and the train/validation split, \emph{not} the test set, so the standard deviation does not capture test-sampling uncertainty (with 785 test samples a single accuracy has a binomial 95\,\% interval of about $\pm2.7$ points). No hyper-parameter was tuned on the test set. All features are extracted once in fp16 autocast and cached; no augmentation is used. Images are normalised with the per-band statistics of the released data (computed without labels over train and test images, as in the original extraction scripts).
