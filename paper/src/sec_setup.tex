\section{Benchmarks and Evaluation Protocol}\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{VertiBench Satellite.}
The Satellite dataset of VertiBench~\cite{wu2024vertibench} contains, for 3{,}927 WorldStrat~\cite{cornebise2022worldstrat} locations, 16 Sentinel-2 L1C revisits (13 bands, $158\times158$ pixels, rescaled to 8 bits) distributed over $K=16$ parties, and a four-class label: the \emph{provider} of the location (Amnesty, ASMSpotter, Landcover, UNHCR; the majority class covers 59.9\,\% of the test set). We use the public train/test files as is: 3{,}142 training and 785 official test samples; 15\,\% of the training samples are held out for validation with the seed. The split is fixed by the benchmark, so seeds change the initialisation and the validation subset, not the test set. These 16 parties observe the same sensor at different dates: this is multi-view fusion rather than a cross-silo split over different feature spaces.

\paragraph{Recovering the locations of VertiBench.}
VertiBench does not ship coordinates, but its samples are WorldStrat locations. We match each VertiBench sample to a WorldStrat location by the similarity of the DINOv2-g features of their 16 revisits (VertiBench L1C vs.\ WorldStrat L2A images, mean over revisits, each data set centred), with a single global one-to-one assignment that ignores the labels. The labels are only used afterwards, as a check: the provider of the matched location agrees with the VertiBench label for \geoagree\,\% of the samples, \geonconfident{} of the \geonsamples{} samples are mutual best matches, and for matched pairs the $p$-th VertiBench party is closest to the $p$-th WorldStrat revisit in \geoalign\,\% of the cases (chance: 6\,\%), i.e.\ the acquisition dates line up. These coordinates give a location-only baseline on VertiBench and a spatial cross-validation of it.

\paragraph{WorldStrat, sensor-level split.}
To obtain parties with genuinely different data, we also use the SPOT (1.5\,m, pan-sharpened) and Sentinel-2 (L2A, 10\,m, 16 revisits) images of the 3{,}925 usable WorldStrat locations: one party holds SPOT, a second party holds the Sentinel revisits (or sixteen parties hold one revisit each). We use the publicly released DINOv2 ViT-g/14 class tokens of the RGB bands of both sensors (each image standardised before encoding) and three labels: the provider (\texttt{sources}, 4 classes), the IPCC land cover (7 classes, strongly imbalanced; the few locations without an IPCC class count as ``Other'') and a 4-class simplification of it.

\paragraph{Random and spatial cross-validation.}
For WorldStrat and for the pooled VertiBench samples, we compare two five-fold cross-validations with the same fold roles (fold $f$ is the test set, fold $f{+}1$ the validation set, the rest the training set), so that the \emph{test set changes with the fold} in both: a stratified \emph{random} partition, and a \emph{spatial} one in which the locations are clustered by $k$-means on their 3D unit-sphere coordinates ($k{=}25$; $k\in\{10,50\}$ as a sensitivity check) and whole clusters are assigned to balanced folds. A location-only random forest (500 trees on longitude and latitude) and the majority class of the training folds are evaluated under the same protocols.

\paragraph{Frozen encoders.}
All frozen encoders see the RGB bands (B4, B3, B2) standardised per band with the statistics of the released data (computed without labels on all images), bilinearly resized, and are used through their pooled class token: DINOv2 ViT-S/B/L/g~\cite{oquab2024dinov2} at $168$\,px ($12\times12$ patches) and, as a control, at $224$\,px; CLIP ViT-L/14 and ViT-B/16~\cite{radford2021clip}, SigLIP ViT-B/16~\cite{zhai2023siglip} and a supervised ImageNet ResNet-50 at $224$\,px; a randomly initialised ViT-B~\cite{dosovitskiy2021vit} as a floor; four false-colour composites through DINOv2-g covering six bands (B2--B4, B8, B11, B12); and the Sentinel-2 model SSL4EO-S12 ViT-S/16~\cite{wang2023ssl4eo} on all 13 bands. Features are extracted once in half precision.

\paragraph{Baselines trained on the raw images.}
All baselines use the full-resolution raw images ($158\times158$ pixels), either all 13 bands (as the published reference) or the RGB bands. \emph{VertiBench recipe}: we re-implement the published SplitNN configuration (one ResNet-18 per party with a $9\times9$ stride-2 input convolution, MLP aggregation $[1000,4]$, Adam with learning rate $10^{-5}$ and weight decay $10^{-5}$, step decay every 10 epochs, batch 32, 50 epochs, no early stopping; inputs scaled to $[0,1]$), train it on the whole training set and test the last epoch. \emph{SplitNN, our recipe}: standard ResNet-18~\cite{he2016resnet} bottoms (only the number of input channels changed) trained from scratch, or initialised from ImageNet for RGB, and an MLP top model identical to our head, trained for 40 epochs (Adam, one-cycle schedule with peak learning rate $2\cdot10^{-3}$, $3\cdot10^{-4}$ with ImageNet initialisation, weight decay $10^{-4}$, batch 64, random flips, mixed precision); the checkpoint with the lowest validation loss is tested. \emph{One-shot with party-side SSL}, a SimCLR~\cite{chen2020simclr} variant of FedOnce~\cite{wu2022fedonce} (which uses noise-as-targets): each party pre-trains its own ResNet-18 from scratch with NT-Xent (temperature 0.2, two random resized $128\times128$ crops with flips, rotations, per-band gain/offset jitter and noise; 100 epochs, batch 256) on its unlabeled training images, uploads the 512-d embedding of each whole image once, and the active party trains the same head as for the frozen features; the pre-training is run once, so its seeds only vary the head. Times are wall-clock on one NVIDIA A100-40GB GPU, data loading included.

\paragraph{Training protocol and statistics.}
All heads use Adam (lr $10^{-3}$, batch 512), at most 50 epochs, \texttt{ReduceLROnPlateau} (factor 0.3, patience 4) and early stopping on the validation loss (patience 10); the checkpoint of the best validation loss is tested. These settings were fixed before the experiments and are shared by all encoders. We report accuracy and macro one-vs-rest AUC as mean $\pm$ sample standard deviation over five seeds or folds. On the fixed VertiBench test set the seed-to-seed spread does not capture test-sampling uncertainty (a single accuracy around 80\,\% has a binomial 95\,\% interval of $\pm2.8$ points with 785 samples); key comparisons are therefore made on the \emph{same} test predictions with a paired bootstrap and an exact McNemar test. Operating points (compression level, dropout rate) are selected on the validation set.
