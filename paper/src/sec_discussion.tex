\section{Decision Guide and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Which approach under which constraint.}
Start from one upload of frozen foundation features, and depart from it only for a binding constraint. \emph{Bandwidth, or parties online only once:} compress the upload with a local PCA (on VertiBench, \selTwokBytes{} per sample for \selTwokAcc\,\%). \emph{Accuracy, if every party can train and stay online:} split learning with LoRA (on MILK10k, \fzGapViewsCb{} to \fzGapViewsB{} F1 points over one upload with the same backbone, for \fzUpdinovTwoblora{} per sample; no significant gain on VertiBench). \emph{No embedding at the server, many rounds acceptable:} logit heads, whose leakage we did not measure. \emph{A formal guarantee:} DP noise on a PCA upload, loose, valid only if the PCA and clipping are fixed without the protected data, and at a cost in accuracy. \emph{Individual vectors hidden:} the encrypted ridge on PCA uploads, at a cost in accuracy and compute, revealing $X^\top X$ and $X^\top Y$. Secret transforms of the upload are no substitute.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The frontier is the envelope of the methods we ran, with untuned baselines, on two main benchmarks of one domain each; DP was evaluated only on VertiBench. VertiBench parties are redundant, its siblings confounded with class, and only \holmSurvive{} of \holmN{} paired tests survive a Holm correction; the two satellite benchmarks share source areas; MILK10k lacks patient identifiers and its gains from adaptation depend on the recipe. Alignment is assumed complete, the attacks are lower bounds and the encrypted head is emulated.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
Image VFL does not have to keep every party online for thousands of messages. Public foundation encoders turn FedOnce into a strong default: each party runs a frozen encoder, compresses with its own PCA and uploads once, and the label owner trains alone. A few dozen bytes per sample match split learning on satellite images, a few kilobytes reach the best trade-off we measured, and compression also lowers the price of differential privacy and of encrypted training. Interaction keeps a place: on dermatology images, adapting the encoder buys a few points, at the price of keeping every party online. We measured this on data whose parties really hold different views---satellite revisits and two photographs of a lesion---and release these benchmarks, with a two-sensor satellite task, under a common protocol. We hope new image VFL methods will be judged the same way: on natural benchmarks, against one upload of frozen features, by what each extra message buys.
