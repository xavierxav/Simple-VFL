\section{Decision Guide and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Which regime under which constraint.}
On our benchmarks, the binding constraint selects the regime as follows.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Parties can be online once, or bandwidth binds:} one upload of frozen features compressed by local PCA (R1). On VertiBench, \selTwokBytes{} per sample give \selTwokAcc\,\% in a single round, and \frVbBeatNonFoundBytes{} already match the reproduced SplitNN. On MILK10k, frozen DINOv2-g features averaged over 8 views and fused per party give \mrGLateViews\,\% macro F1 for \frMilkBestOneShotBytes.
\item \emph{Accuracy binds and the domain is far from pre-training:} split learning with LoRA (R3), if every party can run backpropagation and stay online. On MILK10k it gains \fzGapCb{} to \fzGapB{} F1 points with the same head, for \fzUpdinovTwoblora{} per sample and a round per mini-batch; on VertiBench it gains nothing.
\item \emph{The server must not receive embeddings, and rounds are acceptable:} the four-logit head (R2), \lgClipAcc\,\% on VertiBench for \lgClipUp{} over about \lgClipEp{} epochs. We have not measured what its logits leak.
\item \emph{A formal guarantee is required:} DP noise on a PCA upload, with a loose per-upload guarantee and an accuracy cost (\dpStSsf\,\% at $\varepsilon=64$ per upload on VertiBench).
\item \emph{Individual vectors must stay hidden:} a ridge head trained under CKKS on PCA uploads (R4), accepting an accuracy loss, a compute cost that grows with $D^2$, and the disclosure of $X^\top X$ to the server.
\end{itemize}
Secret party-side transforms are not a substitute for these guarantees, and the plaintext upload must be treated as linkable whenever the server may hold copies of the images.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The frontier is empirical: it is the envelope of the methods we ran, on two main benchmarks that each cover one domain, and our split-learning and FedOnce baselines follow published or standard recipes without extensive tuning. A compressed frozen ImageNet encoder, which might join the low-byte end of the envelope, was not tested, and on MILK10k the four-logit head and DP uploads were not run. The VertiBench parties observe the same sensor at different dates and are largely redundant, and \auSibShare\,\% of its test samples have a training sibling, confounded with class; our paired tests use three seeds and are uncorrected for multiplicity. MILK10k has no patient identifiers and few lesions of the rarest classes per fold, and its frozen-versus-adapted gaps depend on the training recipe. On WorldStrat, the second sensor adds little. All federations are fully aligned; entity resolution, partial overlap and late-joining parties are not studied. The privacy results are lower bounds from specific attackers, the trained baselines were not attacked, the DP budgets are loose, and the encrypted head is emulated in plaintext with operation timings rather than an end-to-end protocol.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
We asked when one upload is enough in image VFL, and made two contributions. (C1)~With a common accounting of bytes, rounds, party compute and server exposure, every point of the accuracy--communication envelope we measured on VertiBench Satellite and MILK10k uses a foundation encoder. One upload of frozen features compressed by local PCA is the knee: on VertiBench, \frVbBeatNonFoundBytes{} per sample match reproduced split learning with \frVbBeatNonFoundRatio{} times fewer bytes, and \selTwokBytes{} reach \selTwokAcc\,\%. Adapting the backbone buys accuracy where the domain is far from pre-training (MILK10k) and not elsewhere. A few-round logit head trades rounds for a smaller exposure. Local PCA is what makes the private end affordable, through DP with a loose guarantee or a ridge head under CKKS that hides individual vectors, both at a cost in accuracy. (C2)~Natural benchmarks change the conclusions that split CIFAR suggests: VertiBench Satellite, reproduced and audited; MILK10k, two photographs of the same lesion; and WorldStrat, whose site-grouped folds remove an optimistic bias and whose second sensor adds little. We suggest that image VFL methods report their position on this frontier, on such benchmarks, with the same accounting.
