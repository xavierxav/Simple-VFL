\section{Decision Guide and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Which regime under which constraint.}
With a foundation encoder as the default, the binding constraint selects the regime. \emph{Bandwidth, or parties online only once:} one upload compressed by local PCA (R1; on VertiBench, \selTwokBytes{} per sample for \selTwokAcc\,\%). \emph{Accuracy, if every party can train and stay online:} split learning with LoRA (R3; \fzGapViewsCb{} to \fzGapViewsB{} F1 points over the strongest frozen pipeline on MILK10k for \fzUpdinovTwoblora{} per sample, no significant gain on VertiBench). \emph{No embedding at the server, rounds acceptable:} the four-logit head (R2), whose own leakage we did not measure. \emph{A formal guarantee:} DP on a PCA upload, loose and at an accuracy cost. \emph{Individual vectors hidden:} a ridge head under CKKS on PCA uploads (R4), at a cost in accuracy and compute, revealing $X^\top X$. Secret transforms are no substitute.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The frontier is the envelope of the methods we ran, with untuned baselines, on two main benchmarks of one domain each; we did not compress a frozen ImageNet encoder, nor run the four-logit head and DP on MILK10k. VertiBench parties are redundant, its siblings confounded with class, and only \holmSurvive{} of \holmN{} paired tests survive a Holm correction; the two satellite benchmarks share source areas; MILK10k lacks patient identifiers and its freezing gaps depend on the recipe. Alignment is assumed complete, the attacks are lower bounds and the encrypted head is emulated.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
We asked when one upload is enough in image VFL. (C1)~With a common accounting of bytes, rounds, party compute and server exposure, foundation encoders are the best default: every point of the measured accuracy--communication envelope on VertiBench Satellite and MILK10k uses one. On VertiBench, a \frVbReachSplitNNFoundBytes{} upload per sample reaches the mean accuracy of reproduced split learning with \frVbReachSplitNNFoundRatio{} times fewer bytes, and \selTwokBytes{} reach the knee (\selTwokAcc\,\%). Adapting the backbone gains a few points on MILK10k and no significant accuracy on VertiBench; local PCA makes DP and an encrypted ridge head affordable, at a cost in accuracy. (C2)~Natural benchmarks (VertiBench reproduced and audited, MILK10k, a two-sensor WorldStrat task whose stratified folds overstate macro F1 by 6 to 9 points) show margins and complementarity far smaller than split CIFAR suggests. Image VFL methods should report their position on this frontier, on such benchmarks, with the same accounting.
