\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Vertical federated learning and communication.}
In vertical federated learning (VFL) several parties hold different features of the same samples and only one of them holds the labels~\cite{liu2024vfl}. The reference training scheme is split learning~\cite{vepakomma2018splitnn}: each party runs a local \emph{bottom} model, sends its activations to the label owner at every mini-batch, and receives gradients back, so that the communication cost grows with the number of epochs, samples and parties. It is reduced by local updates (FedBCD~\cite{liu2022fedbcd}), by compressing the exchanged embeddings at every round (C-VFL~\cite{castiglia2022cvfl}), or by exploiting non-overlapping samples~\cite{kang2022fedcvt}. The most radical option is \emph{one-shot} VFL: FedOnce~\cite{wu2022fedonce} lets every passive party learn a representation of its own data without labels, uploads it once, and trains the top model at the label owner; Sun et al.~\cite{sun2023oneshotvfl} reduce communication to one or a few rounds with semi-supervised learning on limited overlapping samples. The price is that every party must pre-train an encoder on (often small) local data. Open libraries such as VFLAIR~\cite{zou2024vflair} and benchmarks such as VertiBench~\cite{wu2024vertibench} make these methods comparable; FeT~\cite{wu2024fet} and LASER-VFL~\cite{valdeira2025laser} relax the alignment and full-participation assumptions that we keep, and \cite{wu2025vflpractice} documents how far typical benchmarks are from deployable settings.

\paragraph{Pre-trained backbones and foundation models.}
Pre-trained backbones are hard to beat in \emph{horizontal} federated learning on images~\cite{hu2023flimages}; training only the last layer on frozen features~\cite{legate2023guiding} or sharing parametric summaries of foundation-model features in a single round~\cite{beitollahi2024fedpft} removes most of the training and communication cost there. Vision foundation models trained on natural images (DINOv2/v3~\cite{oquab2024dinov2,simeoni2025dinov3}, CLIP~\cite{radford2021clip}, SigLIP~\cite{zhai2023siglip}) and Earth-observation models trained on satellite data (SSL4EO-S12~\cite{wang2023ssl4eo}, SatMAE~\cite{cong2022satmae}, Scale-MAE~\cite{reed2023scalemae}, CROMA~\cite{fuller2023croma}, DOFA~\cite{xiong2024dofa}, RemoteCLIP~\cite{liu2024remoteclip}) provide strong frozen features for remote sensing~\cite{lacoste2023geobench}, with results that depend markedly on input resizing and normalisation~\cite{corley2024resizing}. We study frozen features as the \emph{single upload of a vertical split}: its accuracy and cost against split learning and SSL-based one-shot VFL on the same data, the compression of that upload without coordination, missing parties, and what the upload reveals about the images and their location.

\paragraph{Privacy of uploaded representations.}
Uploading intermediate representations is not private by itself: inversion attacks reconstruct inputs from image representations~\cite{mahendran2015understanding,dosovitskiy2016inverting} and from the activations of collaborative inference~\cite{he2019modelinversion}, and VFL and split learning are exposed to feature, label and hijacking attacks~\cite{luo2021feature,fu2022label,pasquini2021unleashing}. Formal guarantees such as Gaussian differential privacy~\cite{dong2022gdp,dwork2014algorithmic} require noise levels that are usually costly in utility. We measure leakage empirically, with a decoder trained by the label owner on each upload format, and we pair it with the accuracy and the formal guarantee of the \emph{same} clipped Gaussian mechanism.

\paragraph{Geography: evaluation and implicit geolocation.}
Random train/test splits of georeferenced samples overestimate performance when nearby samples share labels, which is why spatial block cross-validation is recommended~\cite{roberts2017crossvalidation,ploton2020spatial,meyer2021aoa,karasiak2022spatial,rolf2024mission}. Image representations also encode \emph{where} an image was taken: CLIP-based models are strong image geolocators~\cite{haas2023streetclip,haas2024pigeon,vivanco2023geoclip}, and location encoders can be learned from satellite imagery~\cite{klemmer2025satclip}. Both facts matter for the WorldStrat-based benchmarks~\cite{cornebise2022worldstrat} used here, whose labels are partly determined by where the images were taken.
