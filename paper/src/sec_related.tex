\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Vertical federated learning and communication.}
In VFL several parties hold different features of the same samples and one of them holds the labels~\cite{liu2024vfl}. The reference training scheme is split learning~\cite{vepakomma2018splitnn}: each party runs a \emph{bottom} model, sends its activations to the label owner at every mini-batch and receives the gradients back. Communication is reduced by local updates (FedBCD~\cite{liu2022fedbcd}), by compressing the exchanged embeddings (C-VFL~\cite{castiglia2022cvfl}), by ADMM-based training (VIMADMM~\cite{xie2024vimadmm}) or by exploiting non-overlapping samples~\cite{kang2022fedcvt}. \emph{One-shot} VFL goes further: in FedOnce~\cite{wu2022fedonce} every passive party learns a representation of its data without labels (Noise As Targets~\cite{bojanowski2017nat}) and uploads it once; Sun et al.~\cite{sun2023oneshotvfl} need three messages and semi-supervised learning to cope with few aligned samples. Libraries and benchmarks such as VFLAIR~\cite{zou2024vflair} and VertiBench~\cite{wu2024vertibench} make methods comparable, but image benchmarks are mostly built by cutting one image into pieces (CIFAR, MNIST) or rendering several views of an object (ModelNet~\cite{wu2015modelnet,su2015mvcnn}); VertiBench Satellite, built from WorldStrat~\cite{cornebise2022worldstrat}, is one of the few with naturally distinct parties. FeT~\cite{wu2024fet} and LASER-VFL~\cite{valdeira2025laser} relax the alignment and participation assumptions that we keep, and \cite{wu2025vflpractice} discusses the gap between benchmarks and practice.

\paragraph{Pre-trained and foundation models in federated learning.}
Pre-trained backbones are hard to beat in \emph{horizontal} federated learning~\cite{hu2023flimages}: training only the last layer on frozen features~\cite{legate2023guiding} or sharing, in a single round, class-conditional parametric summaries of foundation-model features (FedPFT~\cite{beitollahi2024fedpft}) removes most of the training and communication. These summaries require labels at every client, which the passive parties of VFL do not have. Frozen foundation models as VFL party encoders appear in recent work: Split-MoPE~\cite{irureta2026splitmope} uploads frozen DINOv2 embeddings once and trains a mixture of experts for missing parties, VAFL~\cite{huang2026vafl} fits closed-form heads on frozen ViT features with additively homomorphic encryption, and Jin et al.~\cite{jin2026secure} train on secret-shared frozen ResNet features in a three-server MPC; all evaluate on patch-split CIFAR-like images. We study the same principle on naturally partitioned data, against reproduced and published VFL methods, and measure its cost, the price of freezing, the complementarity of real parties and the leakage of the upload. Vision foundation models (DINOv2~\cite{oquab2024dinov2}, CLIP~\cite{radford2021clip}, SigLIP~\cite{zhai2023siglip}), Earth-observation models (SSL4EO-S12~\cite{wang2023ssl4eo}) and medical image--text models (BiomedCLIP~\cite{zhang2023biomedclip}, MONET~\cite{kim2024monet}) provide the encoders.

\paragraph{Privacy of uploaded representations and encrypted training.}
Uploaded representations are not private by themselves: images can be reconstructed from their features~\cite{mahendran2015understanding,dosovitskiy2016inverting} and from the activations of collaborative inference~\cite{he2019modelinversion}, and VFL is exposed to feature, label and hijacking attacks~\cite{luo2021feature,fu2022label,pasquini2021unleashing}. Formal protection comes from differential privacy~\cite{dwork2014algorithmic,dong2022gdp} or from cryptography. Linear and logistic models can be trained on vertically partitioned data with additively homomorphic encryption~\cite{hardy2017private} or secure computation of the Gram matrix~\cite{gascon2017privacy}, and on data encrypted once with approximate homomorphic encryption (CKKS) without further interaction~\cite{kim2018logistic,han2019logistic}; HETAL~\cite{lee2023hetal} trains a softmax head on encrypted foundation-model embeddings in the single-owner setting. These protocols are mature for \emph{linear} models; we measure what a linear head costs in accuracy on frozen features, and therefore what encrypted one-shot VFL would cost.
