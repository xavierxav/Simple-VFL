\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{VFL and its communication.}
Split learning~\cite{vepakomma2018splitnn} exchanges bottom activations and their gradients at every mini-batch~\cite{liu2024vfl}. Communication is reduced by local updates~\cite{liu2022fedbcd}, compressed embeddings~\cite{castiglia2022cvfl}, ADMM~\cite{xie2024vimadmm} or non-overlapping samples~\cite{kang2022fedcvt}. In one-shot FedOnce~\cite{wu2022fedonce}, each party learns a representation without labels~\cite{bojanowski2017nat} and uploads it once; Sun et al.~\cite{sun2023oneshotvfl} need three messages and semi-supervised learning. FeT~\cite{wu2024fet} and LASER-VFL~\cite{valdeira2025laser} relax the alignment and participation assumptions that we keep. VFLAIR~\cite{zou2024vflair} and VertiBench~\cite{wu2024vertibench} make methods comparable, but their image tasks mostly cut one image into pieces or render views of an object~\cite{wu2015modelnet,su2015mvcnn}; \cite{wu2025vflpractice} discusses this gap with practice.

\paragraph{Foundation models in federated learning.}
Pre-trained backbones are hard to beat in horizontal federated learning~\cite{nguyen2023where,hu2023flimages}, where training only the last layer~\cite{legate2023guiding} or sharing class-conditional feature summaries once (FedPFT~\cite{beitollahi2024fedpft}) removes most of the communication; such summaries need labels at every client, which VFL parties lack. In VFL, Split-MoPE~\cite{irureta2026splitmope} uploads frozen DINOv2 embeddings once and handles missing parties with a mixture of experts, VAFL~\cite{huang2026vafl} fits closed-form heads on frozen ViT features under additively homomorphic encryption, and Jin et al.~\cite{jin2026secure} train on secret-shared frozen ResNet features. All three use patch-split CIFAR-like images. We compare regimes on naturally partitioned data against reproduced baselines, and measure the price of freezing, the cost of each regime and the exposure of the upload. Our encoders are DINOv2~\cite{oquab2024dinov2}, CLIP~\cite{radford2021clip}, SigLIP~\cite{zhai2023siglip} and domain models~\cite{wang2023ssl4eo,zhang2023biomedclip,kim2024monet}.

\paragraph{Leakage and encrypted training.}
Images can be reconstructed from features~\cite{mahendran2015understanding,dosovitskiy2016inverting}, from split activations~\cite{he2019modelinversion} and, with generative decoders, even from CLIP and self-supervised embeddings~\cite{bordes2022rcdm,ramesh2022unclip,zheng2026rae,lei2025drag}; VFL faces data, feature, label and hijacking attacks~\cite{jin2021cafe,luo2021feature,fu2022label,li2022labelleakage,pasquini2021unleashing}. Formal protection comes from differential privacy~\cite{dwork2014algorithmic,dong2022gdp} or cryptography. Linear models can be trained on vertically partitioned data with additively homomorphic encryption~\cite{hardy2017private}, a secure Gram matrix~\cite{gascon2017privacy}, or data encrypted once with CKKS~\cite{kim2018logistic,han2019logistic}, with threshold keys for several parties~\cite{mouchet2021multiparty}; HETAL~\cite{lee2023hetal} trains a softmax head on encrypted foundation-model embeddings of a single owner.
